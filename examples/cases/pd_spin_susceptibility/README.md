# Pd: spin susceptibility and Stoner enhancement

Paramagnetic Pd close to ferromagnetism: bare (Pauli), on-site Stoner and LSDA-on-the-grid spin susceptibilities, to compare with the QE fixed-spin-moment ladder of the README.

## Run it

- **PAOFLOW Studio**: Setup tab, *Examples…*, pick this case and a folder name. Studio copies
  the inputs and the pseudopotentials, fills the **run** form and lists the runs below in the
  Runs tab.
- **Command line**: `paoflow-gen --example pd_spin_susceptibility` copies the case into `./pd_spin_susceptibility` and writes the
  drivers; then run the steps below in that folder.

## Steps

1. Self-consistent run: `pw.x -in scf.in`
2. Non-self-consistent run on the full k grid: `pw.x -in nscf.in`
3. PAOFLOW: `mpirun -np 4 python main.py`, then `python plot.py` (or the Plots tab)

Pseudopotentials (from `PSEUDOS/library`): `Pd_ONCV_PBE_sr.upf`.

## Notes from the original example (`examples/qe_examples/example18`)

# Example 18: the spin susceptibility of Pd

This example computes the uniform static spin susceptibility χ = dM/dh of fcc Pd, a nearly ferromagnetic metal, in
three ways and compares them with a fixed-spin-moment calculation in Quantum ESPRESSO. Here M is the spin moment per
cell and h = g_s μ_B B/2. The three methods are:

1. **bare**: the frozen PAO bands, the Pauli term 2N(E_F).
2. **stoner**: the on-site LSDA mean field of the PAO shells. The Stoner integrals come from the radial functions and
   the LSDA spin kernel (`stoner_parameters`, `self_consistent_spin`).
3. **alda**: the LSDA spin kernel acting on the magnetization density on the real-space grid of the crystal.

## Running it

```bash
mpirun -np 8 pw.x -nk 4 -in scf.in > scf.out
mpirun -np 8 pw.x -nk 4 -in nscf.in > nscf.out
mpirun -np 8 python main.py                  # main.py was generated by paoflow-gen (property: spin susceptibility)
cd fsm && for f in M*.in; do mpirun -np 8 pw.x -nk 4 -in $f > ${f%.in}.out; done && cd ..
python fsm_susceptibility.py                 # chi from the fixed-spin-moment ladder
```

The QE runs use LDA (PZ), the norm-conserving PBE pseudopotential `Pd.upf` (from `PSEUDOS/nc-sr-05_pbe_standard_upf`) and
Marzari–Vanderbilt smearing of 0.02 Ry. `main.py` uses the same width, `SPIN_DEGAUSS = 0.272` eV. The fixed-spin-moment
inputs leave their scratch in `fsm/tmp`; delete it afterwards.

## Reference results

Mac Studio, 8 MPI ranks. The PAOFLOW run (DOS and the spin response at 16³ k-points) takes 40 s.

| method | χ (μ_B/eV per cell) | enhancement χ/χ₀ | effective Stoner I = 2(1/χ₀ − 1/χ) (eV) |
|---|---|---|---|
| bare, 16³ | 2.24 | 1 | |
| on-site Stoner, 16³ | 4.88 | 2.2 | 0.48 |
| LSDA on the grid, 16³ | 15.8 | 7.0 | 0.77 |
| QE fixed spin moment, 24³ | 14.3 | 6.3 (χ₀ = 2.26 at 24³) | 0.74 |

- **bare.** χ₀ equals 2N(E_F) of the QE nscf eigenvalues on the same 12³ grid to five digits.
- **grid LSDA.** Its effective I is converged in k to 1 % and lies within 3 % of QE's.
- **Sensitivity of χ.** Pd is close to the Stoner instability (I·N ≈ 0.86), so χ itself amplifies every difference in
  χ₀. At 12³ the same I gives χ = 27. Converge the k-grid before quoting χ.
- **on-site model.** It leaves out the bond part of the magnetization density and its self-consistent shape, and gives
  about 2/3 of the LSDA exchange. Use it for trends, for spin-orbit runs and for ordered moments; it gives
  2.11 μ_B for bcc Fe against QE's 2.15.

The validation of Pd, Pt and Fe is in `docs/internals/spin-response.md`.
